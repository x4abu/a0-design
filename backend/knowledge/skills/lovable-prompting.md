# Prompting like a designer (Lovable-style practice)

Source: patterns from Lovable's public prompting guidance and community practice.

What makes an AI design tool produce good work:
1. Brief, don't wish. Name the product, the audience, the job, the feeling, and the
   constraints ("a 3-screen onboarding for a Jaipur ceramics studio, warm, quiet,
   no stock photography").
2. One change at a time. Iterate with a single, named change per turn instead of
   "make it better".
3. Give references in words: "like a paper journal", "like a museum label", "like a
   train timetable". References carry more design information than adjectives.
4. Ask for the system, not the screen: palette, type, spacing, component states.
5. Lock what works: once a palette and type pairing is right, freeze it and iterate
   on layout only.
6. Keep the model honest about states: loading, empty, error, and the worst-case data.

In a0: the composer's Plan mode asks two questions, then locks the theme before
generating — the tool does steps 4–5 for the user.
